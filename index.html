What is balcony solar?

A small solar panel that clamps to a balcony railing and plugs into a regular outlet. The electricity it produces flows back into the apartment’s circuit, offsetting whatever’s drawing power at that moment.

1

A panel on your railing

Typical kits are 400–800W — one or two solar panels that mount on the outside of a balcony railing or sit on a stand. No roof, no permits in most of Europe.

2

Plugs into any outlet

A small micro-inverter converts the DC output to standard 120V AC and feeds it into the apartment’s wiring through a normal outlet. No electrician required.

3

Offsets your bill in real time

Every watt the panel produces is one less watt the grid has to supply. Whatever’s on at the moment — fridge, AC, laptop — uses the panel’s electricity first.

Common questions

Is plug-in solar legal in NYC?+

Not yet. New York currently requires utility approval for any device that pushes power back into a household circuit, which plug-in kits do. The SUNNY Act (S8512/A9111) would change that — it passed the State Senate 62–0 in 2026 and is awaiting Assembly action. Once it’s law, you wouldn’t need Con Edison’s permission. Renters and co-op residents may still need a landlord or board’s sign-off, though — the bill addresses the utility side, not the lease side.

Does it work for any apartment?+

Any NYC address with sun on at least one side. Production varies a lot — a high-floor south-facing balcony can produce 3× what a low-floor north-facing one does. The 3D simulation accounts for neighbor buildings within 200m, so wide-avenue and midblock exposures get treated differently.

How does it actually plug in?+

The panel connects to a small micro-inverter with a cord ending in a standard 120V plug — same as anything else you plug into a household outlet. NYC balconies almost never have an outdoor outlet, so in practice the cord runs from the panel through a window or sliding door to an indoor outlet — often the same circuit you’d use for a window AC. The cord should be secured to the railing so it doesn’t catch on anyone, and shouldn’t be daisy-chained through an extension cord; kit manufacturers sell longer panel cables for runs over ~10 feet.

How accurate is this estimate?+

Within roughly ±12–18% of real-world production for a typical NYC apartment. The biggest sources of uncertainty are micro-shading (a neighbor’s AC unit, a corner tree) and exact panel orientation. We model major shadows on your block but can’t catch every obstruction.

How is this calculated?+

PVWatts hourly simulation, NYC building data, and a 3D shadow model of your block. Built on NREL, NYC PLUTO, Con Edison SC-1 rates, and EPA eGRID. Read the full methodology →

What does a kit cost?+

A few hundred dollars for a small panel, up to a couple thousand for a larger kit with battery storage. Most NYC-sized setups land between $1,200 and $1,800.

What rate is used to compute savings?+

Con Edison’s residential rate — roughly 34¢/kWh in 2026, about 56% above the U.S. average. The Customize panel lets you adjust your monthly bill and the long-term rate escalation assumption.
