1Is plug-in solar legal in Virginia?

Virginia has signed a plug-in solar law (HB 395 / SB 250) that takes effect on 1 January 2027. Until then, the utility’s interconnection rules still apply.

Virginia’s device rights start on 1 January 2027, and the law spells out the order of steps. Before installing, the customer sends the utility a State Corporation Commission notification form, online or by mail, giving name, address, account and meter numbers, the device’s model and the laboratory that certified it; in Virginia Beach that utility is Dominion Energy. The utility has 15 days to raise any inaccuracy or gap; if it does not respond, the notice counts and the device can go in. It cannot require approval or a fee, but it may install an automatic locking disconnect where a property has more than one device or the circuit requires it. Devices are outside net metering.

Renters get a qualified right. A landlord who owns more than four rental units may not prohibit a tenant from installing a device on the exterior of the tenant’s premises, but may set reasonable limits on size, place and manner. The tenant gives at least seven days’ notice with documentation and the proposed location, is responsible for any damage, and needs the landlord’s written approval for anything that alters wiring. Tenants of smaller landlords get no such right, and tenants billed through a ratio utility billing system may not install one at all. The law leaves condominium, cooperative and homeowners’ association instruments intact.

Only devices of 391 W or less are exempt from product listing provisions that would require changes to a building’s wiring or panel; the 800 W system balco.nyc models gets no such exemption.

The identical chaptered acts (HB 395 = Ch. 1052, SB 250 = Ch. 998, both approved Apr 22, 2026 at the reconvened session) add Va. Code §56-596.8 (numbered §56-596.7 in the chaptered acts) and §55.1-1212.1 and amend §15.2-2288.7, §56-594 and related sections. Devices are excluded from net energy metering and from the definition of eligible customer-generator. Utilities may not require approval or fees, but the customer must file an SCC notification form before installing. The utility has 15 days to respond, and utilities may install a locking disconnect in some cases. Landlords owning more than 4 units may not prohibit tenants from installing on the exterior of the tenant's premises (reasonable size, place and manner limits allowed; 7 days' notice required; excludes RUBS-billed units). Localities may not prohibit devices on residential structures. HOA, condo and POA covenants are expressly NOT overridden. On the effective date, trackers said July 1, 2026 or Jan 1, 2027. Enactment clause 4 makes the first enactment (all Code changes) effective Jan 1, 2027. Enactments 2-3 (the SCC form due Sept 1, 2026 and a safety work group) take the default July 1, 2026 date (Va. Const. art. IV §13). So both tracker dates are partly right, but the device rights do not start until Jan 1, 2027.

3What it would save, and when it pays back

Virginia’s average residential price over the twelve months to June 2026 was 16.3¢/kWh, below the US average of 17.9¢, according to the U.S. Energy Information Administration. That is an average: it spreads fixed monthly charges across every kWh, so it can differ from what a kWh you no longer buy actually saves. The calculator lets you enter the rate from your own bill instead.

Payback assumes every kWh is used at home, a 3% annual rise in the electricity price and 0.5% a year of panel degradation, with no discounting. With the $1,200 mid-range kit an east- or west-facing balcony pays back in 13.0 years and a north-facing one in more than 25 years. A household using Virginia’s average of 1,032 kWh a month would cover about 5% of its electricity with a south-facing panel.

4Emissions

Virginia Beach is on EPA’s SERC Virginia/Carolina grid subregion (SRVC), which emitted 0.593 lb of CO₂ per kWh generated in 2023 (eGRID2023). A south-facing panel producing 654 kWh a year would avoid about 388 lb of CO₂.

5Common questions

Is balcony solar legal in Virginia?

Virginia has signed a plug-in solar law (HB 395 / SB 250) that takes effect on 1 January 2027. Until then, the utility’s interconnection rules still apply. HB 395/SB 250 (Chs. 1052/998): plug-in solar up to 1,200 W is exempt from interconnection; utility notice before install; tenant and locality protections. Takes effect Jan 1, 2027.

How much would a balcony solar panel produce in Virginia?

About 654 kWh a year for an unshaded 800 W system on a south-facing railing in Virginia Beach, 487 kWh facing east or west, and 201 kWh facing north, in balco.nyc’s PVWatts model. Shade from buildings or trees opposite lowers it; the calculator models that for your address.

How much would balcony solar save in Virginia?

About $107 a year for a south-facing panel at Virginia’s average residential price of 16.3¢/kWh, with a payback of about 10.0 years on a $1,200 kit. Savings assume all the power is used at home.

Does Virginia pay for surplus balcony solar power?

Surplus power earns nothing: the law excludes these devices from net metering.

Do renters in Virginia need permission to install balcony solar?

Yes, in part: the law limits a landlord’s or association’s power to refuse. The details are in the notes above.

Do I have to tell Dominion Energy before installing balcony solar in Virginia?

Yes, once the law takes effect on January 1, 2027. Virginia Code 56-596.8 requires the customer to submit the State Corporation Commission's notification form to the utility, online or by mail, before installation. The utility has 15 days to respond about deficiencies; if it does not, the customer may install. It cannot require approval or charge a fee.

Can a Virginia landlord ban balcony solar?

A landlord who owns more than four rental units cannot prohibit a tenant from installing a small portable solar device on the exterior of the tenant's premises from January 1, 2027, but may set reasonable restrictions on size, place and manner. The tenant must give seven days' notice with documentation and the proposed location. Smaller landlords are not covered, and tenants in units billed through a ratio utility billing system may not install one.
