Germany, by the numbers

What Germany's balcony solar boom tells the US, in registry data

Germany’s official register listed 1,512,855 plug-in solar systems in operation on 23 September 2026, about 37 for every 1,000 households. balco.nyc’s compilation of that register shows the boom has leveled off, with about 450,000 registrations in each of 2024 and 2025 and 2026 running 11% behind, while the systems themselves grew: panels on a new system average about 1,390 W this year, against 775 W in 2023. At Germany’s rate the US would have about 4.9 million, but the wiring, prices and law it would take to get there differ in ways that matter.

1The count: 1.5 million, and no longer growing

Germany keeps the record the United States lacks. German law requires every plug-in solar system, down to a single panel on a railing, to be entered in the Marktstammdatenregister (MaStR, “market master data register”), run by the Bundesnetzagentur (BNetzA, the Federal Network Agency). The register has its own category for steckerfertige Solaranlagen, “plug-in solar systems”, which it glosses as the so-called Balkonkraftwerk, or “balcony power plant”.

balco.nyc queried the register’s public unit search on 23 September 2026 and compiled the category by the date each system started running and the date it was registered. Capacity is not totaled in the public search, so we summed it by counting systems in narrow size bands, which pins the totals to within about 1%; the few entries above the legal limits are included as entered. The yearly rows include systems since taken out of service. Of 1,598,054 entries in the category, 1,512,855 are marked in operation, 51,637 permanently decommissioned, 5,589 temporarily shut down and 27,973 still planned. Counting only systems still running, by the year they were registered, gives 431,688 for 2024 and 434,505 for 2025, close to the “about 435,000” and “about 430,000” the BNetzA announced each January.

Those two years are the story: growth stopped. Registration dates avoid the lag between switching a system on and entering it, so they give the cleanest trend. From January through August, owners registered 338,366 systems in 2024, 344,486 in 2025 and 307,391 in 2026, 11% fewer than a year earlier. By capacity the fleet is still small beside the country’s rooftops: the systems now running that started before 2026 carry roughly 1.2 GW of panels, about 1% of the 117 GW of solar the BNetzA counted in Germany at the end of 2025.

2What the 2024 rules changed

The rule changes usually summarized as “Germany legalized balcony solar in 2024” were several steps, and it helps to date each one. balco.nyc checked each against the statute or the regulator’s own announcement:

In forceChangeSource
1 Apr 2024Registering a balcony system in the MaStR takes five facts about the device instead of about 20, and the BNetzA passes the entry to the local grid operator itself.BNetzA announcement, 28 Mar 2024
16 May 2024The Solarpaket I (“solar package I”) amends the Renewable Energy Sources Act (EEG). A Steckersolargerät (“plug-in solar device”) of up to 2 kW of panels and 800 VA of inverter output in total, behind a household’s meter and giving its surplus to the grid without payment (unentgeltliche Abnahme), may be connected; the grid operator may not demand any notice beyond the register entry. EEG § 8(5a)Law of 8 May 2024, BGBl. 2024 I Nr. 151
16 May 2024Such a device may run on whatever meter is already installed, even an older one without a reverse lock, and that meter’s readings are presumed correct until the meter operator fits a two-way meter (Zweirichtungszähler), which it must do promptly once the register entry is passed on for checking. EEG § 10aSame law
16 May 2024The 600 VA limit for simplified connection in the grid-connection rule VDE-AR-N 4105 falls away.VDE FNN
17 Oct 2024Tenants and condominium owners gain a claim to permission (section 4).Law of 10 Oct 2024, BGBl. 2024 I Nr. 306
Dec 2025 (scheduled)First product standard, DIN VDE V 0126-95: 800 VA; up to 960 W of panels on an ordinary household plug, up to 2,000 W with a special energy plug.VDE / DKE

Two points get lost in translation. The 800 VA figure defines which devices get the simplified route; a larger system is not banned, but it goes through the ordinary connection process with the grid operator. And the surplus is given away by default, as under the signed US laws, none of which pays for exports; the BNetzA notes that an owner may instead claim the regular feed-in payment, but the simplified route in § 8(5a) applies only to devices whose surplus is unpaid. The safety side of the German rules, including the plug requirements, is covered in is plug-in solar safe? This is a summary of statutes, not legal advice.

3What the register shows the rules did: bigger systems, not more of them

If the 2024 rules had a clear effect in the register, it is on size rather than numbers. balco.nyc’s count of new systems by the inverter and panel capacity their owners entered:

Started running600 W inverter800 W inverterPanels over 960 WPanels at 2,000 W
202381.6%8.0%7.8%0.5%
1 Jan – 15 May 202452.5%36.9%21.9%2.0%
16 May – 31 Dec 202417.2%72.3%30.3%4.2%
20256.5%84.9%51.2%20.4%
2026, to 22 Sep3.8%88.7%65.9%34.3%

The inverter switch was under way before the law took effect. The share of new entries listing 800 W rose from 3% in January 2023 to 37% in April 2024, then jumped to 57% in May 2024, the month the new limit applied, and 67% in June. The register records what owners typed in; it cannot show whether an earlier 800 W unit was set to deliver only 600.

Panels grew more steadily and further. In 2026, 2,000 W is the most common panel size entered, and the average new system carries about 1,390 W of panels behind a 765 W inverter, a ratio of 1.8. The inverter clips the midday peak, and the extra panel raises output in the mornings, evenings, winter and shade. Two-thirds of 2026 systems have more than the 960 W the product standard allows on an ordinary plug; the register does not record plug type, so it cannot say how many use a special plug or were bought before the standard.

The count moved less clearly. Registrations from January to May 2024 were 48% above the same months of 2023; from June to December 2024, after the new limits, they were 68% higher. Then, from June to December 2025, they fell 12% below the same months of 2024. The acceleration coincides with the rules, but also with the April registration change and the summer season, and we cannot separate them. Nor is there a visible step after the tenant right arrived in October 2024: 2025 finished 1% below 2024.

4The tenant right: BGB § 554, and the condo rule beside it

Since 17 October 2024, section 554 of the German Civil Code (Bürgerliches Gesetzbuch, BGB) has read, in our translation: “The tenant may demand that the landlord permit structural alterations of the rented property that serve use by people with disabilities, the charging of electric vehicles, protection against burglary or the generation of electricity by plug-in solar devices.” The last clause was added by a law of 10 October 2024, which took effect the day after its publication on 16 October.

It is a right to permission, not a right to install without asking. The claim does not exist where the alteration “cannot reasonably be expected” of the landlord, even weighing the tenant’s interests; the tenant may offer an extra security deposit; and any lease term that takes the right away is void. The same law added plug-in solar to the alterations every condominium owner can demand under § 20(2) of the Condominium Act (WEG), which must be “appropriate”, with the owners’ association deciding by resolution how the work is done.

A tenant right reaches more people in Germany than it would in the US. Fewer than 42% of German households live in a home they own (Destatis, 2022). In the US, 34.7% of households rent: 46.1 million of 132.7 million in the Census Bureau’s 2024 American Community Survey.

The US equivalent is thin. Of the nine signed state plug-in laws, four give tenants or owners some right to install, and only Vermont’s is in force; New York’s SUNNY Act has passed both chambers and awaits the Governor's signature, and grants no such right. The details are in the US laws compared, balcony solar for renters and condos, co-ops and HOAs. The German register cannot tell us what the right achieved, because it does not record whether an owner rents. This is not legal advice in either country.

5Per household, and the systems nobody registered

Destatis, the federal statistics office, counted 41,126,000 households in Germany in 2025 (first results). On that base, balco.nyc’s compilation gives 36.8 registered systems in operation per 1,000 households, one for every 27 households. Using systems still running today, by the date they were registered, the rate has climbed like this:

The table holds households at the 2025 count throughout and leaves out systems registered and later decommissioned, so earlier rates are slight underestimates. The plausible US equivalent is arithmetic, not a forecast: at 36.8 per 1,000, the 132.7 million US households would have about 4.9 million systems. How many US households have a balcony or other outdoor space that could hold one is a separate question, answered in how many US households could use balcony solar.

Every German figure here is a floor. HTW Berlin, a university of applied sciences that researches plug-in solar, estimated in February 2025 that 1.5 to 4 million devices were on the grid, when the register held a little over 862,000. It is a wide range, derived from HTW’s 2022 market research rather than a count; that 2022 study, based on a survey of sellers, found only 10 to 20% of devices registered at the time, when owners also had to notify the grid operator. We found no measurement of the gap since registration was simplified, so we used registered figures only.

The US has no national register to measure against. State laws require notice to the utility in some cases (Maryland and Virginia before installing, Maine within 30 days above 420 W) and none in others, so an American count, if anyone compiles one, will be assembled from utility notices, state by state.

6What carries over to the US, and what does not

Germany shows what happens when the paperwork is removed and electricity is expensive: registered systems reached one for every 27 households within four years, then the count leveled off while the systems grew. It does not show the same thing would happen under American wiring, prices and law. The differences, side by side:

The current is the difference that matters most for safety. The same wattage draws about twice the current at 120 V: an 800 W system is 6.7 A in the US against 3.5 A in Germany, and the common US cap of 1,200 W puts nearly three times Germany’s current onto a smaller circuit. German experience with 800 VA therefore describes a smaller load than the one US laws allow; the circuit arithmetic is in is plug-in solar safe?

Price is the difference that matters most for demand. Both price figures are averages that include fixed charges. At the European Central Bank’s reference rate on 23 September 2026, $1.1411 per euro, Germany’s figure is about 46 US cents: roughly 2.6 times the US average, above the average in every US state (Hawaii’s 42.75¢ included, in balco.nyc’s state data), and above the 34¢ Con Edison marginal rate balco.nyc uses for New York City (why that rate). The German boom happened at prices few American households pay; the closest parallels are the high-price states, not the average. What an 800 W system would produce and save at your address is what the calculator estimates.

7Common questions

How many balcony solar systems are there in Germany?

Germany's official register, the Marktstammdatenregister, listed 1,512,855 plug-in solar systems in operation on 23 September 2026, with about 1.07 GW of inverter capacity and 1.65 GW of panels, by balco.nyc's compilation. That is about 37 for every 1,000 households. It is a floor: HTW Berlin estimated in February 2025 that 1.5 to 4 million devices were on the grid when about 862,000 were registered.

Is balcony solar still growing in Germany?

The number of new systems has leveled off. About 450,000 were registered in each of 2024 and 2025, and registrations from January to August 2026 were 11% below the same months of 2025. The systems are getting bigger instead: new systems in 2026 average about 1,390 W of panels, against 775 W in 2023.

What is Germany's balcony solar law?

Since 16 May 2024, section 8(5a) of Germany's Renewable Energy Sources Act lets a plug-in solar device of up to 2 kW of panels and 800 VA of inverter output, giving its surplus to the grid unpaid, be connected without any notice to the grid operator beyond an entry in the national register. It may run on the existing meter until a two-way meter is fitted. Since 17 October 2024, tenants and condominium owners have a claim to permission to install one. This is a summary, not legal advice.

Can German renters install balcony solar without their landlord's permission?

No. Section 554 of the German Civil Code, amended in October 2024, gives tenants a right to demand the landlord's permission, which the landlord can refuse only where the installation cannot reasonably be expected of them after weighing the tenant's interests. Lease terms that take the right away are void. The US has nothing comparable nationally; among the states only Vermont's right to install is in force as of September 2026.

Why is the German limit 800 W when US laws allow 1,200 W?

The numbers are not comparable in watts alone. German homes run at 230 V, so 800 VA is about 3.5 A, 22% of a typical 16 A circuit. US outlets run at 120 V, so 1,200 W is 10 A, two-thirds of a 15 A circuit: nearly three times the current on a smaller circuit, which is why German safety experience does not transfer directly.

If the US matched Germany, how many plug-in solar systems would it have?

At Germany's rate of 36.8 registered systems per 1,000 households, the 132.7 million US households counted by the Census Bureau in 2024 would have about 4.9 million systems. That is a scale marker, not a forecast: the US has cheaper electricity on average, more owner-occupied houses and, in most states, no plug-in law in force.
