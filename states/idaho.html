Balcony solar in Idaho

A plug-in solar bill in Idaho (H 612) did not become law. The utility’s interconnection rules apply, which usually means approval is required. An 800 W balcony system on a south-facing railing in Boise would produce about 714 kWh a year in balco.nyc’s PVWatts model, worth about $89 at Idaho’s average residential price of 12.5¢/kWh (50th highest of 51), which pays back a $1,200 kit in about 11.7 years.

1Is plug-in solar legal in Idaho?

A plug-in solar bill in Idaho (H 612) did not become law. The utility’s interconnection rules apply, which usually means approval is required.

An archived copy (2026-03-13) of the official bill page shows H 612 (by the State Affairs Committee) introduced on 2/10/2026 and referred to State Affairs on 2/11/2026. Nothing further happened; secondary trackers say it was never heard. The legislature adjourned sine die on April 2, 2026, and Idaho has no carryover. legislature.idaho.gov refused connections from this environment, so I could not read the bill PDF. The 1,200 W cap, UL/NRTL certification, and exemption from interconnection and net metering come from search summaries of the official PDF. Whether it had landlord or HOA provisions is unverified. Conflict: pluginsolarmap says 'no bill filed yet', which is wrong. Cap/certification/right fields describe the bill's last version, not law.

3What it would save, and when it pays back

Idaho’s average residential price over the twelve months to June 2026 was 12.5¢/kWh, below the US average of 17.9¢, according to the U.S. Energy Information Administration. That is an average: it spreads fixed monthly charges across every kWh, so it can differ from what a kWh you no longer buy actually saves. The calculator lets you enter the rate from your own bill instead.

Payback assumes every kWh is used at home, a 3% annual rise in the electricity price and 0.5% a year of panel degradation, with no discounting. With the $1,200 mid-range kit an east- or west-facing balcony pays back in 15.1 years and a north-facing one in more than 25 years. A household using Idaho’s average of 944 kWh a month would cover about 6% of its electricity with a south-facing panel.

4Emissions

Boise is on EPA’s WECC Northwest grid subregion (NWPP), which emitted 0.632 lb of CO₂ per kWh generated in 2023 (eGRID2023). A south-facing panel producing 714 kWh a year would avoid about 451 lb of CO₂.

5Common questions

Is balcony solar legal in Idaho?

A plug-in solar bill in Idaho (H 612) did not become law. The utility’s interconnection rules apply, which usually means approval is required. H 612 (portable solar exemption from utility rules) was referred to House State Affairs and never heard. It died when the legislature adjourned sine die on April 2, 2026.

How much would a balcony solar panel produce in Idaho?

About 714 kWh a year for an unshaded 800 W system on a south-facing railing in Boise, 532 kWh facing east or west, and 196 kWh facing north, in balco.nyc’s PVWatts model. Shade from buildings or trees opposite lowers it; the calculator models that for your address.

How much would balcony solar save in Idaho?

About $89 a year for a south-facing panel at Idaho’s average residential price of 12.5¢/kWh, with a payback of about 11.7 years on a $1,200 kit. Savings assume all the power is used at home.

Does Idaho pay for surplus balcony solar power?

Idaho has no plug-in solar law, so there is no rule for it; connecting a device that exports power normally needs the utility’s approval first. The estimates here assume surplus power earns nothing.

Do renters in Idaho need permission to install balcony solar?

No plug-in solar law gives a right to install one, so a renter needs the landlord’s permission and a condo or co-op resident the board’s, unless a general solar-access law covers the balcony.
